Search animals by common or scientific name. Photos come from iNaturalist. Relationships come from the Open Tree of Life. Tap an animal for its photo credit and source links.

The tree is the Open Tree of Life pruned to the species you picked, so the order of splits stays the order from that tree. The drawing starts on a synthetic ultrametric scale. When this page is opened through serve.py, DateLife looks up published chronograms and, if they cover every species, replaces those lengths with ages in millions of years. The Open Tree split order stays in place. If no chronogram covers the set, the synthetic scale remains. A species is included only when its photo loads. Randomize draws a number from 5 to 10, skips groups with fewer species than that number, and picks that many species from one phylum, class, order, or family.

The world map shows up to 100 coordinate-checked GBIF records per species. These are a sample of recorded sightings, not a complete range or proof that a species is absent elsewhere. Records may be biased, historical, or include captive animals. iNaturalist supplies example photos; photos are not necessarily from the mapped GBIF records.

Starter taxonomy and sightings were cached on 4 November 2026; additional searches and sightings load online. GBIF name matching requires an exact species-level match; unmatched taxa stay in the tree with an unavailable map. What does this tree show?

This is an educational model. The original dataset contains 340 representatives from Generations I–V, not the whole Pokédex. It includes a 747-character matrix and analysis instructions, but no machine-readable tree. Our backbone is a new neighbor-joining reconstruction from that matrix’s Hamming distances; it is not the paper’s Bayesian tree.

The other 685 species have estimated placements. Missing members of represented game evolution families are attached at their family’s common ancestor. New families are attached beside the most similar archived representative, using types (45%), egg groups (30%), shape (20%), and habitat (5%, when known). Ties use the lowest Pokédex number. Family members form unresolved groups. These choices preserve all relationships among the original 340 tips, but they have not been tested as a biological phylogeny. Game evolution is a transformation, not speciation.

Every tip lines up with “Now.” Node ages use the maximum number of edges to a descendant tip, scaled so the full root is 1 and tips are 0. Subsets retain those ages. The root is arbitrary. This is an ultrametric drawing with synthetic relative time, not dates or measured divergence times.

Maps, sprites & facts

The existing world map by MosseN1998 is an illustrative fan map. Pokémon are grouped by debut generation; positions within regions are for display, not encounter locations. Hisui additions appear with Sinnoh. All selected Pokémon appear together, spread around their regions.

All 1,025 sprites are existing images from PokéAPI: original game sprites and matching Gen 5-style community sprites by Smogon contributors. Source image files are unchanged; the display fits around their transparent margins. No artwork was generated. Forms such as Mega Evolutions are not additional species and are not separate picker cards.

Species facts: PokéAPI. Short biology excerpts: Bulbapedia contributors, credited on each card. Pokémon belongs to its respective rights holders. Selections stay in this browser.
